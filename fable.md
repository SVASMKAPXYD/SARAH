Your question: how does Gemini relate the image to the Three.js world?
It doesn't, natively. Gemini sees pixels and has no knowledge of your camera's focal length, so a pixel column is not a bearing and a pixel row is not a distance. Left on its own it will guess scale and direction, and it has no way to check that guess except by seeing what happens after it moves. So yes, you need a bridge, and a grid is the right shape for it. What I put in §3c:

Shared camera model, exposed as a grid. RGB, thermal and LiDAR all come from one camera pose with one FOV. The LiDAR grid's columns are 10° sectors of that FOV, keyed by relative bearing. That makes "image strip 7 ↔ +20° ↔ 3.8 m to a TREE ↔ turn_deg: 20" a single lookup, and depth comes from LiDAR rather than from Gemini estimating it off the picture. The ground row is what catches logs and water the level row misses.
Burn the grid into the images Gemini receives. Vertical dividers labelled with bearings along the top, the LiDAR distance per column along the bottom, a horizon line, and ground-distance ticks at 2/5/10 m projected with the known camera. This is a few canvas 2D calls after readback and removes any intrinsics math from the model's side. It's the single cheapest, highest-value piece.
Close the loop in text. last_result reports exactly what a command did in world units, and every node in the packet carries a precomputed bearing_from_rover_deg and distance_m. Gemini can calibrate its sense of scale against outcomes and reason about the map without trigonometry.
Two stretch options, if it still misjudges: ask Gemini to return the target's position in its normalized 0–1000 image coordinates and unproject that pixel through the camera onto the terrain (gives you a world point for the UI overlay and a sanity check on its turn_deg), or render Gemini's own graph top-down with the rover arrow and FOV wedge as a third image, which leaks no world truth.

One thing to flag: I set the camera FOV to 90° with 9 sectors rather than the spec's ±60° with 7 named sectors, because 120° through a perspective camera at 512×384 gets fisheye-ish and shrinks the survivor at 10 m, and numeric keys that double as turn angles are more useful to Gemini than names. It's in open question 3 as a constant to confirm at T+0.
